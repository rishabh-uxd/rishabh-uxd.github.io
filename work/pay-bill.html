💳 Credit card servicing

Amazon Visa Pay Bill

Role
UX lead, end to end
Team
PM, me, 15 engineers
Timeline
March 2022, launched October 2023
Impact
23% fewer late fees, 21% less attrition
01

Background

The Amazon Visa is a Chase co-branded card and one of Amazon's most important payment products. Until 2023, customers who wanted to see a statement or pay a bill had to leave Amazon and do it on Chase's site, which most of them did not expect.

About half of the one and two star reviews on the card were about account management, and roughly one in five new cardholders picked up a late fee while they were still waiting for the physical card to arrive. Amazon's S-team decided to bring card management in-house. I led UX for three of those features, and Pay Bill was the first one out.

02

Project goals

Build a transparent and intuitive Pay Bill experience, so that fewer customers incur a late fee, fewer customers close the account because of one, and managing the card feels like the rest of Amazon. The year one targets were a 20% reduction in monthly late fee incidence and a 20% reduction in attrition.

We kicked off in March 2022 with a six month UX timeline and four sub-features in scope:

  • One-time payment. Pick an amount, pick a bank account, pay.
  • Manage bank accounts. Add, verify, remove, set a default.
  • Automatic payments. Enroll, change the amount, cancel.
  • Payment activity. See what is scheduled, pending, and posted.
03

Team and role

A Product Manager, me as the only designer, and 15 engineers. My responsibilities were:

  • Creating interaction design flows
  • High-fidelity mock ups
  • Prototyping
  • Partnering with UX Research on end-to-end usability testing
  • Organizing leadership reviews, both at Amazon and at Chase
  • Accessibility annotations
04

Design and outcomes

I worked the payment experience out in wireframes early, before there were requirements to design against. Amazon used them to write those requirements, and Chase used them to check their APIs and their legal and compliance position. Several things surfaced that way before any engineering started, and one of them cost us the best idea in the project.

Specifically, the slider experience we had proposed had to be axed from the initial release because of an unresolved L&C issue on the Chase side. The slider showed customers the estimated interest for whatever payment amount they selected, in real time, so they could see the cost of their choice as they made it instead of reading about interest in fine print. Usability participants called it something they'd never seen a credit card do. When it was cut, I redesigned the selection experience on a compressed timeline with no room for usability testing, using competitor patterns as a familiarity proxy.

The slider won an Amazon Inventor Award in 2023. Its implementation and a modified version of the design were later used in another project that launched and shipped.

Pay Bill launched in October 2023. The numbers below are from an analysis run in September 2024, about eleven months in.

2.3M One-time payments made

23% Fewer late fees, against a 20% goal

21% Less attrition, against a 20% goal

10% Fewer 1 and 2 star ratings
